<objective>
Wire the two forms that currently cannot receive inline errors at all, because they have no `prop` and — for the mood page — no bound model. This plan implements D-01 (user-visible path) and D-04 (a cancelled confirm is not a failure) for its delete handler.

This is a retrofit, not an attribute addition. `MoodTracker.vue` renders `<el-form label-width="80px">` with no `:model`, so `validate()` cannot run and `:error` has no item to attach to; its four constrained fields are driven by a `reactive` plus a raw button group. `Monitor.vue`'s inline form does have `:model` but not one `prop`, and its add handler pre-checks `metricKey`/`value` with an `ElMessage.warning` before calling the API. Both are why the batch is sequenced here rather than in wave 5: they need structural work, and doing it after the mechanical migrations would leave two pages whose error behaviour is unlike every other form.

The mood page is also the only place where the 5 mood label additions in plan 14-03 are load-bearing. Without them a 400 on `moodScore` renders the camelCase key to the user; with them it renders 心情评分. That makes this plan the verification that the label decision was right.

`Monitor.vue` carries an ERR-05 trap worth naming: its `Object.assign(form, { metricKey: '', value: 0, unit: '' })` sits inside the try after the successful add. A careless edit that moves it out of the try, or into a catch, converts a routine failure into silent data loss on a form the user was actively typing into. The spec asserts the reset does not happen on failure.

Purpose: no form in the application is unable to display a server field error.

Output: two structurally retrofitted forms, both wired to the summary and inline channels, with the raw-key leak and the reset-on-failure trap each pinned by a test.
</objective>
